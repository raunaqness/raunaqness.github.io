Coming to the car itself — the Honda Elevate is a very practical SUV. It is not trying to overwhelm you with an endless list of features or gimmicks. Instead, it focuses on the things that actually matter when you live with a car every day.

The driving position is excellent, visibility is one of its biggest strengths, the cabin feels spacious, and the ride quality is comfortable. The ground clearance gives you confidence on Indian roads, while the size makes it relatively easy to manoeuvre in the city. The boot space is generous enough for family use, and the rear-seat space makes it a comfortable car for longer journeys as well.

The 1.5-litre petrol engine paired with the CVT is not meant to give you a sporty, aggressive driving experience. It is smooth, relaxed and predictable — exactly what you appreciate when the car is being used for daily commuting, city traffic and highway drives. The CVT especially makes stop-and-go traffic quite effortless.

What I particularly like about the Elevate is its overall balance. It feels substantial without being unnecessarily bulky, has a good road presence without being flashy, and is comfortable without trying to be overly luxurious.

Of course, if you are someone who wants a car loaded with every possible feature — multiple gimmicks, connected technology, fancy screens and a long feature list — there are other options in the market that may suit you better.

But if you are looking for a car that is practical, comfortable, dependable, easy to drive, spacious, well-built and designed to be lived with for years, the Elevate makes a very strong case for itself.

For me, a Honda car is about peace of mind, reliability, practicality and the confidence that the car will quietly do its job year after year.

The Elevate, in that sense, feels very much like a Honda.

Not a car bought just for the first impression — but one that you appreciate more the longer you live with it.
